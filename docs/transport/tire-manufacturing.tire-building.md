# Tire Building

> **Node ID**: transport.tire-manufacturing.tire-building
> **Domain**: [Transport](./index.md)
> **Dependencies**: [`transport.tire-manufacturing`](tire-manufacturing.md)
> **Timeline**: Years 20-40+
> **Outputs**: pneumatic_tires
> **Critical**: No

Tire building assembles the uncured ("green") tire on a rotating **building drum** — a precision cylindrical drum that holds the components in sequence while an operator or automated layer applies, stitches, and turns them. For a radial passenger tire, the sequence is: inner liner (butyl sheet), carcass ply (radial, 90 degrees), bead bundles (steel wire hoops set at the drum ends), sidewall and bead filler, then turn-up (the ply edges are folded up and over the beads). The drum then transfers to a second-stage builder where the **steel belts** and tread are applied on a toroidal (inflated) former that gives the crown its final shape.

## Components and Precision

Every layer must be positioned within ±2-3 mm and spliced without overlap gaps or air pockets. Stitcher wheels (rotating metal rollers) press each layer down, expelling trapped air. Tension on the steel belt wire is controlled to ±1%; a loose belt causes high-speed vibration and uneven wear. The bead rings must be concentric to the drum axis within ±0.5 mm or the finished tire will have radial runout (a hop).

## Bead Winding

Bead bundles are wound separately on bead-winding machines — a single bronze-coated steel wire (0.8-1.5 mm) is wrapped in multiple turns with a rubber apex strip to form the rigid hoop that locks onto the wheel rim. Passenger beads use 15-25 wraps; truck and aircraft beads may use 100+ wraps or multiple hoops side by side.
