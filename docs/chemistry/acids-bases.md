Industrial acids and alkalis underpin nearly all chemical processing: ore leaching, metal pickling, glass etching, textile finishing, soap and detergent production, and semiconductor wafer cleaning. The progression runs from wood-ash lye (K₂CO₃) through lime (CaO), soda ash (Na₂CO₃), and on to strong mineral acids and caustic soda (NaOH).

## Mineral Acids

The [Mineral Acid Production](acids.md) article covers all acid processes in detail: the lead chamber and contact processes for sulfuric acid (H₂SO₄), hydrochloric acid (HCl) from salt + H₂SO₄ or direct H₂ + Cl₂ synthesis, nitric acid (HNO₃) from saltpeter or the Ostwald process, and hydrofluoric acid (HF) from fluorspar. It also covers phosphoric acid, aqua regia, pickling acid management, semiconductor-grade acid purification, acid azeotropes, materials of construction, safety protocols, and troubleshooting for all acid processes.

Sulfuric acid alone accounts for 60% of global chemical production by volume — a civilization's industrial maturity can be measured by its H₂SO₄ output. The contact process (96-98% H₂SO₄ via V₂O₅ catalyst) is the backbone route, while the lead chamber process (62-70%) provides a bootstrap alternative that avoids vanadium.

## Alkalis

The [Alkali Production](alkalis.md) article covers soda ash (Na₂CO₃) via the Leblanc and Solvay processes, caustic soda (NaOH) via lime-soda causticization and chlor-alkali electrolysis, potash (K₂CO₃) from wood ash, lime chemistry, and detailed Solvay process step-by-step. It also includes alkali applications in soap, textiles, and paper, waste management, and troubleshooting.

Soda ash is the gateway alkali: the Solvay process converts salt and limestone to Na₂CO₃ with near-complete ammonia recycle. Caustic soda (NaOH) is produced from soda ash by the lime-soda process or directly from brine electrolysis. Before synthetic alkalis, wood-ash potash served for glass and soap at artisanal scale.

## Bootstrap Sequence

1. **Wood ash potash** (Year 1-5): Burn hardwood, leach ash, evaporate to K₂CO₃. For glass and soft soap. Land-intensive.
2. **Lime from limestone** (Year 3-8): CaCO₃ → CaO at 900-1200°C. Slake to Ca(OH)₂ for mortar and causticization.
3. **Lead chamber H₂SO₄** (Year 8-15): From sulfur or pyrite. 62-70% acid for pickling and fertilizer.
4. **HCl from salt + H₂SO₄** (Year 10-15): Leblanc salt cake step, or direct synthesis from H₂ + Cl₂.
5. **HNO₃ from saltpeter** (Year 10-15): NaNO₃ + H₂SO₄ distillation. Limited by nitrate deposits.
6. **Solvay soda ash** (Year 15-25): Requires ammonia. Displaces Leblanc for Na₂CO₃.
7. **Contact process H₂SO₄** (Year 15-25): V₂O₅ catalyst. 96-98% acid. Requires clean, dry SO₂.
8. **Ostwald HNO₃** (Year 20-30): From ammonia and air over Pt-Rh gauze. Requires [ammonia production](ammonia.md).
9. **Electrolytic NaOH** (Year 25+): Chlor-alkali membrane cells. See [Electrolysis](electrolysis.md).

## Acid-Base Safety

- **Sulfuric acid**: Concentrated H₂SO₄ (98%) causes severe thermal and chemical burns. Always add acid to water. Heat of dilution: 880 kJ/kg. PPE: face shield, neoprene gloves, rubber apron.
- **Nitric acid**: Powerful oxidizer. Contact with organics causes fire. Toxic NO₂ fumes. Store in aluminum or glass.
- **Hydrochloric acid**: HCl gas irritates respiratory tract at 5 ppm. Scrub vent gases through NaOH. Store in rubber-lined steel, glass, or PVC.
- **Caustic soda**: NaOH causes deep chemical burns that penetrate tissue. Eye contact with 50% NaOH risks permanent blindness. Emergency shower and eyewash mandatory.
- **Hydrofluoric acid**: Uniquely lethal — penetrates skin, binds calcium, causes cardiac arrest. Calcium gluconate antidote stations required at all HF work areas. See [acids.md](acids.md) for detailed HF safety protocols.
