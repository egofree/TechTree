# Gas Airships

> **Node ID**: aerospace.lighter-than-air.gas-airships
> **Domain**: [Aerospace](./index.md)

> **Outputs**: airships
> **Critical**: No

Gas airships (dirigibles) are powered, steerable lighter-than-air craft using helium or hydrogen for lift. Unlike hot-air balloons, gas airships generate lift from a permanently sealed lifting gas rather than continuous heating, allowing sustained flight for days without fuel expenditure on lift maintenance. Internal combustion or electric motors drive propellers for thrust; movable rudders and elevators on the tail fins provide directional control.

## Structural Types

**Rigid**: An internal framework (historically aluminum, modern concepts use carbon fiber composite) holds the envelope shape and contains individual gas cells. The Zeppelin LZ series are the classic examples. Rigid airships can be built to enormous size (the Hindenburg was 245 m long) but require complex internal structures.

**Semi-rigid**: A partial keel or internal frame reinforces a pressurized envelope. Modern Zeppelin NT designs use this approach, combining a partial internal triangular truss with a helium-filled envelope.

**Non-rigid (blimp)**: Envelope shape is maintained entirely by internal gas pressure slightly above ambient. No internal framework. Simpler and lighter, but limited in maximum size. Used for advertising, surveillance, and small patrol airships.

## Lift Gas and Ballast

Helium is the standard modern lifting gas (safe, non-flammable). Hydrogen provides 8% more lift at far lower cost but introduces severe fire risk. Gas airships carry ballast (water or lead shot) that can be released to offset gas loss through permeation and valve operations. Ballonets (internal air chambers) allow altitude changes without venting lifting gas, by inflating or deflating with air to compensate for gas expansion and contraction.
