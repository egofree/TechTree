## Overview

The crew cabin is the habitable pressure vessel that sustains astronauts in flight: the
atmosphere system, structural shell, seating, windows, and accommodation layout. It must
hold 101 kPa (14.7 psi) against vacuum, survive launch vibration and abort loads, and keep
its crew alive through every phase of the mission.

## Pressure Vessel

A double-walled aluminium-lithium shell: inner pressure vessel (2-4 mm Al-Li, welded) carries
the pressure load; outer Whipple shield (thin bumper spaced 5-15 cm off the inner wall) absorbs
micrometeoroid impacts. Crew Dragon uses an aluminium structural shell; Orion uses Al-Li 2195.

## Atmosphere System

Sea-level-equivalent: 101 kPa, 78% N2 / 21% O2, 20-22°C, 30-70% RH. Five subsystems:
high-pressure O2 tanks, N2 make-up, LiOH or amine CO2 scrubber, condensing humidity
exchanger, and activated-charcoal trace contaminant control.

## Crew Accommodation

- **Seats**: custom-moulded couches (Soyuz fibreglass, Orion recumbent) to distribute g-load
  along the spine during launch and reentry.
- **Windows**: triple-paned fused silica (Orion's 58 cm side windows); Crew Dragon has none.
- **Hatches**: forward docking hatch and side / top ingress-egress hatch.
- **Displays**: touchscreen control (Crew Dragon); physical switch-gear (Soyuz).
