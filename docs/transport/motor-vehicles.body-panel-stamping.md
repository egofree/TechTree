# Body Panel Stamping & Welding

Body panels — the outer skin and inner structural panels of a vehicle — are formed from sheet metal by stamping presses, joined by welding, and protected by multi-layer paint. The body shop is the most capital-intensive part of an automotive plant, dominated by 500–2500 tonne presses and robotic welding cells.

## Sheet Metal Stamping

Steel or aluminum coil is sheared into blanks, then pressed in stages: **draw** (forming the 3D shape), **trim** (cutting the outline), **pierce** (holes for fasteners and access), **flange** (bending edges for stiffness and joining), and **hem** (folding one panel edge over another for a flush, safe outer surface). A complex panel like a door outer may require 5–6 operations in a transfer press line. Draw depth is limited by material formability — deep-draw steel (low carbon, killed steel) can stretch 40–50% before tearing; aluminum 20–30%.

## Welding Methods

- **Resistance spot welding**: Copper electrodes clamp two overlapping panels and pass 10,000–15,000 A for 0.1–0.3 s, melting a 5–6 mm nugget. The dominant joining method — 3,000–5,000 spots per body-in-white. Fast (1–2 s per weld), automatable, no filler metal.
- **MIG/MAG welding**: Continuous wire feed shielded by inert (MIG) or active (MAG) gas. Used for frame structures and thicker joints requiring continuous seams.
- **Laser welding**: A focused beam produces a continuous, narrow weld at 2–5 m/min. Used for roof-to-side-panel joints — water-tight, low heat distortion, minimal flange width (saves weight).

## Painting and Corrosion Protection

Multi-layer system on bare steel: zinc phosphate conversion coat → cathodic e-coat (20–30 μm, immersion, full corrosion base) → primer surfacer (sanded smooth) → base coat (colour) → clear coat (UV-protective gloss). Each layer baked at 120–180°C. Total thickness 90–130 μm. The e-coat is the critical corrosion barrier — without it, bare steel body panels perforate in 2–5 years in road-salt environments.

## Composite Panels

Fibreglass (SMC — sheet moulding compound) and carbon-fibre-reinforced panels are used for low-volume and specialty vehicles. SMC panels are compression-moulded in matched metal dies at 150°C — no stamping press required, but cycle time is 2–4 minutes vs. 5–10 strokes/minute for steel stamping.
