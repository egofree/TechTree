## Overview

Sunn hemp (*Crotalaria juncea*) is a fast-growing tropical and subtropical legume cultivated for both fiber and soil improvement. It produces a bast fiber similar to hemp in strength and appearance, suitable for cordage, paper pulp, and coarse textiles. As a legume, it fixes atmospheric nitrogen through root nodules, making it one of the most effective green manure crops available. A single crop can add 60-120 kg of nitrogen per hectare to the soil in 60-90 days.

The plant grows 1-3 meters tall in 60-120 days, producing stems 1-2 cm in diameter. The fiber is extracted from the bark (bast) of the stem by retting (soaking in water to separate fiber from the woody core). The fiber has a tensile strength of 250-450 MPa, making it serviceable for rope and twine though not as strong as hemp or abaca.

Sunn hemp's dual value is its chief advantage. A single planting produces fiber AND improves the soil for the next crop. In rotation systems, sunn hemp is planted after a grain harvest, grown for 60-90 days, then cut and incorporated into the soil. The nitrogen fixed in the plant tissue becomes available to the next crop as the plant material decomposes. This makes sunn hemp a key tool for sustainable agriculture where synthetic fertilizers are unavailable.

The plant is native to India and Pakistan, where it has been grown for fiber since antiquity. It requires warm temperatures (25-35°C optimal) and moderate rainfall (500-1,500 mm). It is drought-tolerant once established.

Primary outputs: `plant_fiber` for cordage and paper, `green_manure` for nitrogen fixation.

## Prerequisites

### Materials

- Sunn hemp seeds (germination rate above 80%)
- Well-drained soil with pH 5.5-7.5
- Warm climate (minimum soil temperature 20°C for germination)

### Equipment

- Hoe or plow for seedbed preparation
- Sickle or scythe for harvest
- Retting tank, pond, or slow-moving stream for fiber extraction
- Beating mallet or break (flax brake) for separating fiber from woody core
- Hackle (comb with metal teeth) for fiber cleaning

### Knowledge

- Seed rate and planting depth for optimal stand density
- Retting time: how long to soak stems in water for clean fiber separation
- Green manure timing: when to cut and incorporate for maximum nitrogen benefit
- Crop rotation planning: sunn hemp before nitrogen-demanding crops (corn, wheat)

### Infrastructure

- Field prepared for broadcast or row seeding
- Retting water source (pond, tank, or stream with slow current)
- Drying area for retted fiber
- Compost or incorporation method for green manure use

## Process Description

### Fiber Production

1. Prepare seedbed by plowing or hoeing to a fine tilth. Broadcast seed at 25-40 kg per hectare for fiber production (dense stand produces taller, finer stems). Plant at 2-3 cm depth.
2. Seeds germinate in 3-5 days at soil temperature above 20°C. The crop grows rapidly, reaching 1-3 meters in 60-90 days. No fertilizer needed (legume fixes its own nitrogen).
3. Harvest for fiber when the lower pods turn brown (approximately 90-120 days after planting). Cut stems at ground level with a sickle.
4. Ret the harvested stems by submerging in water for 4-8 days. The bacterial action decomposes the pectins binding the fiber to the woody core. Check progress daily by bending a stem: when the fiber separates cleanly from the core, retting is complete.
5. Remove stems from the water and dry in the sun for 1-2 days.
6. Break the dried stems by passing them through a flax brake or beating with a mallet. This crushes the woody core into small pieces (shives) that fall away from the fiber.
7. Hackle (comb) the fiber to remove remaining shives and align the fibers. The clean fiber is now ready for spinning.

### Green Manure Production

1. Plant sunn hemp at 40-60 kg seed per hectare (denser planting for green manure).
2. Allow the crop to grow for 60-90 days, until flowering begins. Do not wait for seed set.
3. Cut the crop at ground level with a sickle or scythe, or mow with a blade.
4. Incorporate the cut plant material into the soil by digging or plowing. The green matter decomposes in 2-4 weeks, releasing nitrogen and organic matter.
5. Plant the next crop (grain, vegetable, or other) immediately after incorporation.

## Safety Considerations

- Sunn hemp seeds and foliage contain pyrrolizidine alkaloids that are toxic to livestock and harmful to humans if ingested in quantity. Do not graze animals on sunn hemp fields. Do not consume seeds or foliage.
- Retting water becomes foul-smelling and contaminated with bacteria. Avoid contact with retting water on open wounds. Wash hands after handling retted stems.
- Sunn hemp pollen is a respiratory allergen. Wear a mask during harvest when pollen is present.
- The plant can become weedy in some environments. Prevent seed set in green manure crops by cutting before pods mature.

### Personal Protective Equipment

- Dust mask during harvest and fiber processing
- Gloves for retting and fiber breaking
- Waterproof boots when working in retting tanks or ponds

## Quality Control

### Acceptance Criteria

- **Fiber grade 1**: Light colored (cream to pale yellow), clean, free of shives, uniform length
- **Fiber grade 2**: Light brown, minor shive residue, shorter fiber length
- **Green manure**: Cut at early flower stage before seed set, incorporated within 48 hours of cutting

### Testing Methods

- Visual inspection for color, cleanliness, and residual shives
- Strength test: pull a fiber bundle with hand force. Good fiber resists breaking. Brittle fiber indicates over-retting.
- Retting completeness: bend a stem 90°. If fiber separates cleanly from the woody core without excessive force, retting is complete.
- Nitrogen content of green manure: leaf tissue analysis shows 2.5-4.0% nitrogen at early flower stage.

## Scaling Notes

- **Household garden** (0.01 hectare): 10 m² produces enough fiber for household cordage and returns 1-2 kg of nitrogen to the soil.
- **Field scale** (1 hectare): 500-1,200 kg of fiber plus 60-120 kg of nitrogen returned to soil. Can be harvested and retted by 3-5 workers over 2 weeks.
- **Rotation integration**: Sunn hemp fits between grain crops. Plant wheat, follow with sunn hemp (60-90 days), then plant the next grain or vegetable crop with nitrogen provided by the sunn hemp residue.
- **Bottleneck**: Retting requires reliable water access. In arid regions, dew retting (laying stems in the field for morning dew to moisten them) takes longer (2-3 weeks) and produces inferior fiber.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Probable Cause | Solution |
|---------|---------------|----------|
| Stems too short for fiber | Low plant density or poor soil | Increase seed rate; ensure soil phosphorus is adequate |
| Fiber weak and breaks | Over-retted (soaked too long) | Reduce retting time; check stems daily after day 3 |
| Fiber does not separate from core | Under-retted | Extend retting time; check water temperature (colder = slower) |
| Retting water foul and black | Stagnant water, too many stems | Reduce stem density in retting tank; change water between batches |
| Seed set before green manure cutting | Planted too early or variety too fast-maturing | Cut at early flower regardless of seed set; select later-maturing varieties |
| Nitrogen fixation poor | Soil already high in nitrogen, or rhizobia absent | Inoculate seeds with appropriate rhizobia; avoid fields recently fertilized |

## Variations and Alternatives

- **Hemp** (*Cannabis sativa*): Stronger fiber (550-900 MPa), temperate climate, dual fiber and seed use. Does not fix nitrogen. The premier temperate fiber crop.
- **Jute** (*Corchorus olitorius*): Similar retting process, tropical only. Lower tensile strength but finer fiber suitable for sacking and carpet backing.
- **Kenaf** (*Hibiscus cannabinus*): Fast-growing, dual fiber types (bast and core). Used primarily for paper pulp. Does not fix nitrogen.
- **Cowpea** (*Vigna unguiculata*): Inferior fiber but excellent nitrogen fixation and edible seeds. Better choice if food production is the priority over fiber.
- **Alfalfa** (*Medicago sativa*): Perennial nitrogen fixer. No fiber value, but superior long-term soil improvement. Grows for 5-10 years from one planting.
