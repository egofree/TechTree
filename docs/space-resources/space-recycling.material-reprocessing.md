Reprocessing of metal and polymer scrap generated in orbit into usable feedstock for in-space manufacturing, with unrecoverable mass disposed via destructive cargo-vehicle reentry. This process converts the linear waste stream of expendable cargo disposal into a closed materials loop.

## Solar Concentrator Melting

In space, sunlight arrives continuously at 1,361 W/m² with zero atmospheric attenuation. A 2-3 m diameter parabolic concentrator achieves spot temperatures of 1,500-2,500°C — sufficient to melt aluminum (660°C), steel (1,400-1,500°C), and titanium (1,668°C). The energy is free, unlike electric arc furnaces. The challenge is managing molten metal in microgravity, where surface tension produces floating droplets; electromagnetic confinement or capillary-channel crucibles define the melt geometry.

## Reprocessing Stages

| Stage | Operation | Output |
|-------|-----------|--------|
| Sorting | Magnetic + density separation | Separated metal/polymer streams |
| Compaction | Hydraulic press | Dense briquettes |
| Solar melting | Concentrated solar flux (700-1,700°C) | Molten metal pool |
| Casting | Chill mould or additive feed | Ingot or wire feedstock |
| Polymer extrusion | Heated extruder (180-260°C) | 1.75 mm filament spool |

## In-Space Manufacturing Feedstock

Reclaimed polymer filament from cargo bags and packaging (Kevlar, Nomex, polyethylene) feeds fused-deposition manufacturing of tools, brackets, and replacement parts. Reclaimed aluminum and titanium wire feeds electron-beam or arc additive manufacturing of structural trusses. A single ISS increment generates roughly 1,000-1,500 kg of trash; at 60-70% recoverable content that is 600-1,000 kg of usable feedstock per crew rotation.

## Cargo Vehicle Disposal

Truly unrecoverable mass — hazardous compounds, contaminated materials, end-of-life hardware — is compacted, sealed, and loaded into an expendable cargo vehicle (Cygnus, HTV, Progress) for destructive reentry burn-up over uninhabited ocean. This is the controlled disposal path that prevents orbital debris accumulation and closes the logistics loop by removing waste mass from the orbital regime.
