# Vertical Assembly Building

The Vehicle Assembly Building (VAB) at Kennedy Space Center is the canonical facility for vertical integration of orbital launch vehicles. At 160m tall with a 218m × 158m footprint, it encloses 3,665,000 m³ of conditioned volume — one of the largest enclosed spaces in the world. The structural frame is 98,500 tonnes of ASTM A-36/A-441 steel, designed to withstand Category 5 hurricane winds (200 km/hr), founded on 4,225 steel pipe piles driven 49m to bedrock. The VAB contains four high bays (each with a 139m-tall stacking cell and a 45m × 12.5m door aperture) and one transfer aisle. Lifting is performed by two 175-tonne and one 325-tonne bridge cranes, rated for 10,000-tonne balanced tandem lifts with laser-guided positioning at ±6 mm accuracy. Interior environment is maintained at 22°C ±5°C and 50% ±10% RH by four 5,000-kW air handling units (12-18 MW peak).

## Process Overview

1. **Foundation**: Drive 4,225 pipe piles to bedrock at 49m depth; cap with reinforced concrete pile caps
2. **Structural frame**: Erect 98,500 t of structural steel, braced for 200 km/hr wind
3. **Crane installation**: Mount bridge cranes on 4 runways; commission laser-guided positioning
4. **Enclosure**: Install 45m-high high bay doors; seal envelope for HVAC conditioning
5. **Environmental systems**: Install 4 × 5,000 kW air handlers; commission cleanroom tents at each stacking cell
