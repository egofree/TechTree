## Overview

Fuel cells convert chemical energy directly into electrical energy through electrochemical oxidation of a fuel (typically hydrogen) at an anode and reduction of an oxidant (typically oxygen from air) at a cathode. Unlike a [heat engine](./engine.md), which is constrained by the Carnot efficiency limit, a fuel cell converts chemical Gibbs free energy to electricity without an intermediate thermal step. Theoretical efficiencies reach 83% for hydrogen-air fuel cells at standard conditions, compared to the 40-55% typical of thermal power cycles.

The "SEM Tech" qualifier in this capability node indicates the advanced membrane and electrode manufacturing technologies inherited from [semiconductor-era electrochemistry](../chemistry/sem-tech.md). The five major fuel cell types differ by electrolyte, operating temperature, and catalyst:

**Proton Exchange Membrane Fuel Cell (PEMFC)** operates at 60-80°C using a perfluorinated polymer electrolyte (Nafion or equivalent) that conducts protons (H⁺). Platinum catalyst on both electrodes. Fast startup, high power density (0.5-1.0 W/cm²), 40-60% electrical efficiency. The dominant type for transport applications and small stationary power.

**Solid Oxide Fuel Cell (SOFC)** operates at 800-1000°C using a ceramic electrolyte (yttria-stabilised zirconia, YSZ) that conducts oxide ions (O²⁻). No precious metal catalyst needed — nickel cermet anode, perovskite cathode. 50-60% electrical efficiency, 85% with combined heat and power (CHP). Internal reforming of methane is possible due to high temperature. Used for stationary power generation.

**Molten Carbonate Fuel Cell (MCFC)** operates at 600-700°C using molten lithium/potassium carbonate electrolyte that conducts carbonate ions (CO₃²⁻). 50-55% electrical efficiency. Commercially deployed in MW-scale stationary plants (FuelCell Energy).

**Phosphoric Acid Fuel Cell (PAFC)** operates at 150-220°C using liquid phosphoric acid electrolyte in a silicon carbide matrix. 36-42% electrical efficiency. The first commercially available fuel cell type (UTC Power PureCell).

**Alkaline Fuel Cell (AFC)** operates at 60-220°C using concentrated KOH electrolyte. High efficiency (60-70%) but extremely sensitive to CO₂ contamination (forms carbonate). Used in space programs (Apollo, Space Shuttle).

Fuel cells complement [batteries](./batteries.md) in the energy storage landscape: batteries store a fixed charge, while fuel cells provide continuous power as long as fuel is supplied. For grid backup, [hydrogen](../chemistry/electrolysis.md) generated by electrolysis during off-peak hours can feed fuel cells during peak demand, creating an electrochemical energy storage chain.

## Prerequisites

### Materials

- [Hydrogen fuel](../chemistry/electrolysis.md) — PEMFC and AFC require >99.95% pure H₂. SOFC and MCFC can internally reform methane (CH₄) or other hydrocarbons. CO tolerance: PEMFC <50 ppm; SOFC tolerates it fully.
- Platinum catalyst (Pt) — PEMFC and PAFC anode and cathode. Loading: 0.1-0.4 mg Pt/cm² per electrode. [Platinum refining](../metals/non-ferrous.md) is energy-intensive and scarce.
- Nickel catalyst — SOFC anode (Ni-YSZ cermet, 50-65 vol% Ni). MCFC anode (Ni-Cr or Ni-Al). Abundant and cheap compared to Pt.
- Perovskite cathode material (LSM, LSCF) — SOFC cathode. Synthesised from La₂O₃, SrCO₃, Mn₂O₃ or Co₃O₄.
- [Ion-conducting electrolyte](../chemistry/electrodialysis.md) — varies by type: PEMFC (Nafion membrane), SOFC (YSZ ceramic, 8-10 μm), MCFC (Li₂CO₃-K₂CO₃ in LiAlO₂ matrix), PAFC (100% H₃PO₄ in SiC matrix), AFC (30-45% KOH).
- [Stainless steel](../metals/iron-steel.md) — interconnect plates (SOFC, MCFC), bipolar plates (PEMFC). SOFC interconnects require high-Cr ferritic stainless (e.g., Crofer 22 APU) for oxidation resistance at 800-1000°C.
- [Carbon/graphite](./electrode-manufacturing.md) — bipolar plate material for PEMFC and PAFC. Graphite is corrosion-proof but brittle; stainless steel or carbon composite alternatives reduce cost and weight.
- [Glass/ceramic sealants](../glass/index.md) — SOFC gas-tight seals between cells and interconnects. Must survive thermal cycling 25-1000°C.

- [Electrolysis (SEM Tech)](../chemistry/sem-tech.md) — membrane electrode assembly (MEA) manufacturing, catalyst deposition, thin-film techniques

- Catalyst deposition equipment — screen printing, spray coating, or sputtering for Pt on carbon support (PEMFC); tape casting or screen printing for SOFC electrodes

- Hydrogen storage and handling — high-pressure cylinders (200-700 bar) or metal hydride storage. See [gas handling](../gas-handling/index.md).

### Knowledge

- Electrochemistry of the hydrogen-oxygen fuel cell: Anode: 2H₂ → 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ (E° = 0 V). Cathode: O₂ + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ → 2H₂O (E° = 1.229 V). Overall: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O, ΔG° = -237 kJ/mol. Theoretical reversible cell voltage E°rev = 1.229 V at 25°C.
- Voltage losses: activation polarisation (kinetic overpotential at electrodes, dominant at low current), ohmic losses (electrolyte resistance + contact resistances, linear with current), concentration polarisation (mass transport limitation at high current). Practical operating voltage: 0.6-0.8 V at rated current density.
- Nafion proton conductivity: requires humidification — protons travel as H₃O⁺ (hydronium) clusters through hydrophilic channels. Dry Nafion is an insulator. PEMFC air and hydrogen must be fully humidified at cell temperature.
- SOFC internal reforming: at 800-1000°C, Ni anode catalyses steam reforming (CH₄ + H₂O → CO + 3H₂) and water-gas shift (CO + H₂O → CO₂ + H₂) directly on the cell, eliminating the need for an external reformer.

### Infrastructure

- **Hydrogen supply** — from electrolysis (see [water electrolysis](../chemistry/water-electrolysis.md)), steam methane reforming, or other [hydrogen production](../chemistry/electrolysis.md) routes
- **Fuel processing** — desulfurisation (ZnO bed, <0.1 ppm S for PEMFC), humidification (bubbler or membrane humidifier for PEMFC), pre-reforming (for hydrocarbon fuels to SOFC)
- **Power conditioning** — DC-DC boost from low stack voltage to useful DC bus, DC-AC inverter for grid interconnection
- **Thermal management** — PEMFC cooling (deionised water coolant loop); SOFC heat recovery (air preheater, CHP heat exchanger)
- **Safety systems** — hydrogen leak detection, ventilation, fire suppression, emergency shutdown

## Process Description

### PEMFC Membrane Electrode Assembly (MEA)

1. **Prepare catalyst ink**: Mix Pt/C catalyst powder (40-60% Pt on Vulcan XC-72 carbon black) with Nafion ionomer solution (5-10% by weight of catalyst) in a water-isopropanol solvent. Ball-mill or ultrasonicate for 30-60 minutes to achieve uniform dispersion.
2. **Deposit catalyst on GDL or membrane**: Apply catalyst ink to gas diffusion layer (GDL) by spray coating, screen printing, or doctor blade. Target Pt loading: 0.1-0.4 mg Pt/cm² per side. Dry at 60-80°C between coats. Alternatively, deposit directly on the membrane (catalyst-coated membrane, CCM method) for better interface contact.
3. **Assemble MEA**: Sandwich the Nafion membrane between anode and cathode catalyst layers (if GDL method) or hot-press CCM with GDL on each side. Hot press at 120-150°C, 5-15 MPa, for 1-5 minutes to bond layers.
4. **Build stack**: Assemble MEAs with bipolar plates (anode flow field on one side, cathode on other) and gaskets. Clamp stack between end plates with tie rods at 1-3 MPa compressive pressure. Install in housing with gas manifolds, humidifiers, and cooling plates (every 1-3 cells).
5. **Condition**: Start with nitrogen purge. Switch to humidified hydrogen and air at low flow rate. Apply a low current (0.1 A/cm²) for 30-60 minutes to hydrate the membrane and break in the catalyst. Ramp to rated power over 2-4 hours.

### SOFC Cell Fabrication (Anode-Supported Planar)

1. **Tape-cast anode support**: Mix NiO + 8YSZ powder (50:50 vol%) with organic binder, plasticiser, and solvent. Cast tape to 500-1500 μm thickness on a Mylar carrier. Dry at room temperature for 2-4 hours.
2. **Deposit electrolyte**: Screen-print or tape-cast thin 8YSZ layer (8-15 μm) on top of the anode tape. Lamination during co-sintering bonds the layers.
3. **Co-fire anode + electrolyte**: Heat at 1-3°C/min to 1400-1450°C, hold 2-4 hours. The anode reduces porosity (NiO → Ni in operation creates additional porosity). YSZ electrolyte densifies to >98% theoretical (gas-tight).
4. **Deposit cathode**: Screen-print LSM paste on the sintered electrolyte. Fire at 1100-1200°C for 1-2 hours to bond cathode without degrading the electrolyte interface.
5. **Assemble stack**: Stack cells with Crofer 22 APU interconnect plates, glass-ceramic sealant, and manifolds. Seal by heating to 850°C under load to fuse the glass sealant. Test for gas leaks at 1.2× operating pressure.

## Scaling Notes

- **Bench scale** (1-100 W): Single 5-25 cm² cell in a test fixture. Validates catalyst loading, membrane selection, and flow field design. Gas supplied from cylinders at low flow rates. Measures polarisation curves and durability.
- **Pilot stack** (0.5-10 kW): 5-50 cells with 100-300 cm² active area. Validates stack design, thermal management, and balance-of-plant (humidifiers, compressors, controllers). This is where degradation rates are measured over 1,000+ hour tests.
- **Commercial system** (50-2,000 kW): Hundreds of cells with 500-2,000 cm² active area. Full balance-of-plant including fuel processing, power conditioning, and CHP heat recovery. MW-scale installations (e.g., FuelCell Energy MCFC, Bloom Energy SOFC) connect hundreds of stacks in parallel.

Key scaling challenges: **PEMFC platinum supply** — a 100 kW automotive stack at 0.3 mg/cm² uses 25-50 g Pt. Scaling to millions of vehicles demands significant platinum supply. Reducing loading to 0.05-0.1 mg/cm² (via nano-structured thin-film catalysts) is an active research target. **SOFC thermal cycling** — repeated heating and cooling from room temperature to 850°C stresses ceramic seals and interconnects, causing cracking and gas leakage. Stationary SOFCs run continuously to avoid this. **Stack sealing** — gas leakage between cells is the dominant failure mode for SOFC and MCFC. Glass-ceramic seals must match the thermal expansion of all joined materials across the full temperature range.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Probable Cause | Solution |
|---------|---------------|----------|
| PEMFC voltage decay (>10 μV/h per cell) | Pt dissolution and agglomeration, membrane chemical degradation (fluoride release), catalyst layer flooding or drying | Maintain full humidification; operate below 0.8 V/cell (above this, Pt corrodes); check membrane fluoride release rate (>0.1 μg/cm²/h indicates end of life) |
| PEMFC cell flooding (voltage drops suddenly at high current) | Liquid water blocking gas diffusion layer, insufficient airflow to cathode, excessive humidification | Increase cathode stoichiometric airflow (2-4×); reduce humidifier temperature; improve GDL hydrophobicity (PTFE treatment 5-20 wt%) |
| PEMFC membrane dry-out (high frequency resistance >0.2 Ω·cm²) | Insufficient humidification, excessive cell temperature, low reactant flow | Increase humidifier dewpoint to within 5°C of cell temperature; reduce cell temperature setpoint; verify gas flow rates |
| SOFC open-circuit voltage below theoretical (>5% low) | Gas cross-leakage through electrolyte pinholes, incomplete seal, cracked cell | Pressurise one side with inert gas and detect leak with manometer; replace leaking cells; inspect seals for cracking after thermal cycling |
| SOFC degradation rate >1%/1000 hours | Interconnect oxidation (Cr poisoning of cathode), glass seal degradation, Ni agglomeration in anode | Apply Mn-Co spinel coating on interconnects; minimise thermal cycling; operate at 750-850°C rather than 900-1000°C to reduce Cr volatility |
| PAFC catalyst poisoning (rapid voltage loss) | CO adsorption on Pt blocking H₂ oxidation sites, sulfur compounds in fuel | Maintain fuel clean-up: preferential oxidation (PROX) reactor to reduce CO to <10 ppm; ZnO bed for sulfur removal to <0.1 ppm |
| Stack gas cross-leak (detectable H₂ in cathode exhaust) | Membrane pinhole (PEMFC), cracked electrolyte (SOFC), seal failure (any type) | Reduce operating pressure to below leak threshold; locate and replace leaking cell or seal; pressurise test individual cells |
| Thermal runaway (hot spot >100°C above setpoint) | Localised fuel starvation causing carbon corrosion (PEMFC anode), or localised oxidant starvation causing partial oxidation and heat release | Ensure uniform gas distribution across cell area; install temperature sensors every 3-5 cells; shut down immediately if ΔT >15°C between cells |

## Safety

- **Hydrogen flammability**: H₂ is flammable at 4-75% in air (explosive at 18-59%). Extremely wide flammability range. Odourless, colourless flame (nearly invisible in daylight). Forced ventilation in all fuel cell enclosures; hydrogen detectors at ceiling level (H₂ rises); inert gas (N₂) purge capability.
- **Carbon monoxide (PAFC, MCFC)**: If reformate fuel is used, CO is present. CO is lethal at 0.1% in air for 1 hour exposure. CO detectors mandatory in fuel processing areas.
- **High temperature (SOFC, MCFC)**: 600-1000°C surfaces cause immediate severe burns on contact. Thermal radiation from stack enclosure. Insulation mandatory; allow 4+ hours cooling before opening enclosure. Thermal gloves and face shield for any hot-zone work.
- **Electrical hazard**: Fuel cell stacks generate high current at low voltage (50-400 A at 20-100 V). Series stacks can reach dangerous voltage levels. DC does not pass through zero crossing, so arcs are harder to extinguish than AC. Use DC-rated breakers and fuses.
- **Corrosive electrolytes**: KOH (AFC) causes severe chemical burns. H₃PO₄ (PAFC) is corrosive. Molten carbonate (MCFC) at 650°C is extremely corrosive. Full chemical PPE for electrolyte handling.
- **Pt catalyst fire**: Fine platinum catalyst powder on carbon support is pyrophoric when dry and exposed to air. Keep wetted until installed in cell. Handle under inert atmosphere if dry.

## Quality Control

### Acceptance Criteria

- **Rated power**: At rated current density, cell voltage ≥0.6 V (PEMFC), ≥0.7 V (SOFC). Stack delivers rated power at ≤ specified fuel and oxidant flow rates.
- **Efficiency**: Measured as DC electrical output ÷ (fuel LHV flow rate). PEMFC: ≥40%. SOFC: ≥50%. Verify by energy balance on reactant/product streams.
- **Degradation rate**: PEMFC <10 μV/h (transport), <5 μV/h (stationary). SOFC <1%/1000 hours. Measured by long-duration test at rated load (1,000-10,000 hours).
- **Gas crossover**: H₂ leakage through membrane <0.1 mA/cm² equivalent current (PEMFC). Measured at open circuit by H₂ concentration in cathode exhaust.
- **Pressure drop**: Anode and cathode gas side pressure drop within ±20% of design. High pressure drop indicates flow channel blockage or flooding.

### Testing Methods

- **Polarisation curve (I-V curve)**: Sweep current from open circuit to maximum, record voltage. Identifies activation, ohmic, and mass transport loss regions.
- **Electrochemical Impedance Spectroscopy (EIS)**: Apply small AC perturbation (1-10 mV) over frequency range 0.1 Hz - 100 kHz. Separates ohmic, charge transfer, and mass transport resistances.
- **Endurance test**: Run at rated load for 1,000+ hours. Measure voltage decay rate. Interrupt periodically for EIS to diagnose degradation mechanism.
- **Thermal cycling test (SOFC)**: Heat to operating temperature, hold, cool to room temperature. Repeat 10-100 cycles. Measure OCV after each cycle; gas leakage indicates seal failure.
- **Gas crossover test**: Pressurise one side, measure pressure decay rate or detect leaked gas in opposite stream with gas chromatograph.

### Sampling Protocol

- 100% of stacks: polarisation curve and leak test before shipment
- 1 stack per batch: endurance test (1,000-10,000 hours)
- Quarterly: teardown inspection of one cell from endurance test — SEM of catalyst, membrane fluoride analysis, electrode porosity

## Variations and Alternatives

### Direct Methanol Fuel Cell (DMFC)

A variant of PEMFC that uses liquid methanol directly as fuel, eliminating hydrogen storage and infrastructure. Lower efficiency (20-30%) and power density (0.05-0.25 W/cm²) due to methanol crossover through the membrane. Used for portable power (laptops, military) where liquid fuel convenience outweighs efficiency penalty.

### Reversible Fuel Cell / Unitised Regenerative Fuel Cell (URFC)

A fuel cell that can also operate in reverse as an electrolyser. In fuel cell mode: H₂ + O₂ → electricity + water. In electrolyser mode: electricity + water → H₂ + O₂. Enables electrochemical energy storage without separate electrolyser and fuel cell units. Higher capital cost per function due to bifunctional catalysts.
