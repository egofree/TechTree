## Overview

Pyrethrum (*Chrysanthemum cinerariifolium*, formerly *Pyrethrum cinerariifolium*) is a perennial daisy native to the Dalmatian region of Croatia, cultivated for the insecticidal compounds (pyrethrins) concentrated in its flower heads. Pyrethrins are fast-acting neurotoxins that kill insects on contact by disrupting sodium channel function in nerve cells. They are among the safest insecticides for mammals, with very low mammalian toxicity (LD50 > 1,500 mg/kg) while being lethal to insects at concentrations of 0.1-1.0%.

Pyrethrins provide rapid knockdown (paralysis within seconds of contact) and kill most flying and crawling insects within minutes. They break down rapidly in sunlight (half-life of hours), leaving no toxic residue. This makes them ideal for treating food storage areas, living spaces, and crops close to harvest.

The plant grows 30-60 cm tall with white daisy-like flowers. It is perennial, producing flowers annually for 3-5 years before declining. Pyrethrum thrives in temperate climates with well-drained, slightly alkaline soils. Major commercial production occurs in Kenya, Tanzania, and Australia, though the plant grows in any temperate region with adequate rainfall.

The pyrethrin content of dried flower heads ranges from 0.5-2.0%, with the highest concentrations in flowers harvested just as they fully open. The six active compounds (pyrethrin I and II, cinerin I and II, jasmolin I and II) vary in ratio depending on growing conditions and variety.

Pyrethrins are the model for synthetic pyrethroids (permethrin, deltamethrin), which are more stable in sunlight and more potent but also more persistent in the environment. The natural compounds break down too fast for some agricultural applications but are ideal for indoor use and direct-contact treatments.

Primary outputs: `pyrethrins` (contact insecticide for insects in storage and living spaces).

## Prerequisites

### Materials

- Pyrethrum seedlings or divisions from established plants
- Temperate climate with well-drained, alkaline soil (pH 6.5-8.0)
- Full sun exposure

### Equipment

- Harvesting baskets or trays
- Drying screens (must be in shade to prevent photodegradation)
- Grinding equipment (mortar or mill)
- Sprayer or dust applicator for insecticide use
- Sealed storage containers (dark, cool)

### Knowledge

- Flower harvest timing (fully open, before petal drop)
- Shade-drying technique to preserve pyrethrins
- Grinding and formulation for insecticide use
- Application methods and safety

### Infrastructure

- Pyrethrum field or garden planting
- Shaded drying area
- Grinding and storage area protected from light

## Process Description

### Flower Harvest and Processing

1. Plant pyrethrum in spring. Space 25-35 cm apart in rows 50-60 cm apart. First flowers appear in the second year from seed, or in the first year from divisions.
2. Harvest flowers when fully open but before the petals begin to brown or drop. This is when pyrethrin content is highest. Pick flower heads by hand or cut with scissors.
3. **Dry flowers immediately in the shade.** Pyrethrins degrade rapidly in direct sunlight. Spread flowers in thin layers on screens in a warm, well-ventilated, shaded area. Dry for 5-10 days until brittle.
4. Store dried flowers whole in sealed, dark containers. Grinding releases pyrethrins and accelerates degradation. Grind only when ready to use.
5. A well-managed planting produces 500-1,500 kg of dried flowers per hectare per year.

### Insecticide Preparation

1. **Flower dust**: Grind dried flowers to a fine powder. Apply directly to insects or dust on stored grain, in beehives (for mite control), or in living spaces. The dust kills insects on contact.
2. **Pyrethrin extract**: Soak 100 g of ground flowers in 1 liter of kerosene, alcohol, or oil for 24-48 hours. Strain through cloth. The liquid contains dissolved pyrethrins. Dilute with water (add soap as emulsifier) for spraying, or use the kerosene solution as a mosquito coil fuel.
3. **Water extract** (less effective): Soak 100 g of ground flowers in 1 liter of warm water for 12 hours. Strain. Add liquid soap as a wetting agent. Spray on insects. This extracts less pyrethrin than solvent methods but requires no special solvent.
4. **Application**: Spray or dust in the early morning or evening when target insects are active. Pyrethrins break down within hours in sunlight, so apply when and where they will contact insects quickly.

## Safety Considerations

- Pyrethrins have very low mammalian toxicity but can cause skin and eye irritation. Avoid direct contact with concentrated extracts.
- Pyrethrins are highly toxic to fish and aquatic invertebrates. Do not apply near waterways.
- Pyrethrins are toxic to bees on direct contact. Apply in the evening when bees have returned to hives.
- Some individuals are allergic to chrysanthemum pollen and may react to pyrethrum dust. Use in well-ventilated areas.
- Cats are sensitive to pyrethrins (deficient in the liver enzyme that breaks them down). Keep pyrethrum products away from cats.
- Kerosene-based pyrethrin extracts are flammable. Store and use away from ignition sources.

### Personal Protective Equipment

- Gloves when handling concentrated extracts
- Dust mask when grinding flowers
- Eye protection when spraying

## Quality Control

### Acceptance Criteria

- **Dried flowers**: White petals, yellow centers, no browning, brittle-dry, no mold
- **Ground powder**: Pale yellow-green, fine texture, chrysanthemum odor

### Testing Methods

- Knockdown bioassay: expose houseflies or mosquitoes to the powder or spray. Effective pyrethrum knocks down 80%+ of insects within 5 minutes.
- Color test: fresh, high-pyrethrin flowers are bright white. Brown or yellowed flowers have degraded pyrethrins.
- Smell: good pyrethrum has a faint, characteristic chrysanthemum scent. Musty or no scent indicates degradation.

## Scaling Notes

- **Household planting** (10 m²): 30-50 plants. Dried flower yield: 1-3 kg per year. Sufficient for household insect control.
- **Field scale** (0.5 hectare): 20,000-30,000 plants. Flower yield: 250-750 kg per year. Community-scale insecticide production.
- **Bottleneck**: The rapid photodegradation of pyrethrins limits outdoor agricultural use to direct-contact applications. For persistent crop protection, neem (which has longer-lasting activity) is more practical.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Probable Cause | Solution |
|---------|---------------|----------|
| Flowers have low pyrethrin content | Harvested too early or too late | Harvest at full bloom before browning |
| Powder does not kill insects | Pyrethrins degraded by sunlight during drying | Dry flowers in shade; grind only when ready to use |
| Plants die in wet soil | Root rot from poor drainage | Plant in well-drained soil; avoid overwatering |
| Flowers mold during drying | Dried too slowly or in humid conditions | Increase airflow; dry in thinner layers |
| Pyrethrin spray separates (oil and water) | Insufficient emulsifier | Add more liquid soap; shake frequently during application |

## Variations and Alternatives

- **Synthetic pyrethroids** (permethrin, cypermethrin): More stable, more potent, more persistent. Require chemical industry.
- **Neem** (*Azadirachta indica*): Broader-spectrum insecticide with growth-disruption action. Slower acting but longer lasting. Tropical.
- **Rotenone** (*Derris elliptica*): Tropical vine root extract. Potent but more toxic to mammals and fish.
- **Tobacco** (*Nicotiana tabacum*): Nicotine is an effective contact insecticide. More toxic to mammals than pyrethrins.
