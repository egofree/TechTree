Biological and physical-chemical hybrid systems that close the life support mass loops toward the 99% recovery target required for interplanetary crewed missions. This process extends baseline ECLSS water and atmosphere recovery into a fully bioregenerative regime.

## MELiSSA Five-Compartment Loop

The European Space Agency's MELiSSA (Micro-Ecological Life Support System Alternative) project defines the reference architecture for bioregenerative loop closure. It is structured as an engineered ecosystem of five compartments:

1. **C1 (Waste/degrading)** — Thermophilic anaerobic bacteria degrade fecal waste and inedible biomass into volatile fatty acids, ammonium, CO2, and hydrogen.
2. **C2 (Nitrifying)** — *Nitrosomonas* and *Nitrobacter* oxidise ammonium to nitrate, the preferred nitrogen source for the photosynthetic compartments.
3. **C3a (Photosynthetic bacteria)** — *Rhodospirillum rubrum* fixes CO2 using the organic acids from C1 as electron donors.
4. **C3b (Photosynthetic algae)** — *Chlorella* and *Spirulina* produce 500-600 g O2/m²/day and edible high-protein biomass.
5. **C4 (Consumer)** — The crew and higher-plant chambers consume O2 and food, returning CO2, water vapour, and waste to C1.

## Algae Bioreactors

*Chlorella vulgaris* and *Arthrospira platensis* (Spirulina) are the principal oxygen-generating species. A high-density flat-panel airlift bioreactor (50-100 g/L culture density) requires 9-12 m² of illuminated surface area to close the oxygen loop for a six-person crew. Photosynthetic efficiency is 3-6% of incident PAR (400-700 nm).

## Higher Plant Chambers

Wheat at 5.5 m²/person is the calorie benchmark. Full diet closure requires 40-60 m² per person of mixed crops (wheat, soybean, rice, potato, lettuce), drawing 10-20 kW of lighting power for a six-crew farm.

## Water Loop Push

Advanced brine dryers (membrane distillation, ionomer drying) push water recovery from the ISS baseline of 93% toward 98-99%, with fecal water recovery added to close the remaining deficit.
